\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A description looks random, beyond whatever noise its substrate really has, to the extent that its current state is not a sufficient statistic for its own future. The micro-closure deficit
\[
\mathsf{CD}_\tau(\Pi)=I(X_t;Y_{t+\tau}\mid Y_t)
\]
measures that failure exactly, through the decomposition
\[
H(Y_{t+\tau}\mid Y_t)=H(Y_{t+\tau}\mid X_t)+\mathsf{CD}_\tau(\Pi).
\]
The deficit is zero for closed packagings, and under full stationary support only for them. It bounds the squared stationary route mismatch, and for Markov substrates it caps what any finite window of packaged memory can recover. In controlled Markov chains, it vanishes on lumpable partitions and tracks route mismatch closely. In a memory benchmark, exact entropies show packaged memory recovering a small, well-defined part of a known deficit, and finite-sample models selected on validation data recover the first two steps of that descent. In a toy hashing experiment, a deterministic map looks random under bounded search only when its inputs carry enough entropy.

Within Six Birds, this gives randomness a definite place. It is not a seventh primitive, and it is neither novelty nor irreversibility. It is the predictive residue of non-closure under a chosen packaging, time scale, and budget, exact in finite models and approachable through observable diagnostics elsewhere. Much of the debate over whether randomness is intrinsic or merely epistemic dissolves once the layer is specified. What remains are concrete questions: which distinctions were kept, which were discarded, and what it would cost to recover them.
