\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The claim in one paragraph.} Fix a substrate, a packaging, and a lag. The uncertainty about the next package then splits exactly into two parts. One is the uncertainty that would remain with the full current microstate. The other is the closure deficit, the information about the future that the package hides. The deficit is zero for closed packagings, and under full support only for them. It controls the stationary route mismatch from above, and for Markov substrates it caps what packaged memory can recover. The three demonstrations show the same pattern from different sides. In the Markov benchmark, the deficit behaves as the theory requires and is closely tracked by route mismatch. In the budget benchmark, memory recovers part of a known deficit, and exact entropies show how much remains out of reach. In the hashing toy, a deterministic many-to-one map looks random to a bounded observer only when its inputs supply enough hidden distinctions. Across these cases, much of what a layer calls noise is predictive structure that the layer has not kept.

\paragraph{What the account separates.} The deficit is not novelty. A layer can have a positive deficit and still be a perfectly useful description. The deficit says only that the package is not a sufficient statistic for one staged step of its own future. Nor is the deficit irreversibility. Entropy production and path-space asymmetry are different quantities with different behavior under coarse-graining \citep{esposito2012stochasticthermodynamicsundercoarsegraining,tsiokos2026sixbirds}. Keeping these apart lets one say precisely which kind of failure a given diagnostic detects.

\paragraph{Randomness has a price, and a ceiling.} The budget benchmark makes the accounting reading concrete. Memory is a resource. Spending it lowers the achievable loss, but only as far as the packaged past carries the hidden distinctions. The exact staircase in \Cref{fig:budget} shows both the purchase and its limit. In that chain, roughly $93\%$ of the deficit is out of reach of the eight-step windows we computed, whereas direct access to the microstate would recover all of it. ``Random at this layer'' is therefore not a single verdict. It depends on what the layer is willing to pay for, and on what payment can buy at all.

\paragraph{Refinement is not automatically better.} One might expect finer packagings always to be more closed. They need not be. The four-cycle of \Cref{rem:nonmonotone} gives an exact counterexample: refining a closed one-package description produces a positive deficit. The auxiliary clustering experiment in \Cref{app:aux-rep} is consistent with this. There, learned partitions with more clusters show larger plug-in deficit estimates, although that experiment is descriptive and has known estimation caveats. Refinement pays only when it captures the distinctions that matter for the dynamics at the chosen lag.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The exact theory here is finite and stationary. Continuous or nonstationary settings will need estimators and approximation arguments rather than exact sums. The route-mismatch bound covers the stationary lift only, and the strong agreement of the uniform lift in our benchmark is empirical. The memory bound needs a Markov substrate, or at least a microstate that screens off the past. The budget benchmark uses one chain and one simulated sequence, and the hashing experiment is a deliberately small toy with an idealized reference. Our formal verification covers the finite KL identities, not the full chain of arguments (\Cref{app:lean}). None of these limits affects the exact statements. They mark where the diagnostics should be read as evidence about the deficit rather than as measurements of it.

\paragraph{Outlook.} The account turns a question that is usually posed metaphysically into one that can be computed. It does not ask whether a process is ``really'' random. It asks which distinctions a description keeps, which it discards, and what it would cost to keep more. In the cases studied here, the part of randomness that a description creates is not removed by declaring it noise. It is relocated, priced, or left unpaid.
